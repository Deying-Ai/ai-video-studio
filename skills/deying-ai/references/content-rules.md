# Content Rules

## Viewer Value

Every script should answer at least one of these questions for the viewer:

- What changes in the real world?
- What can they do differently today?
- What mistake can they avoid?
- What should they save for later?
- What deserves a comment or a comparison?

Prefer concrete consequences over vague excitement.

## Structure

Use this sequence unless the source material clearly requires another structure:

1. Hook: a contradiction, cost, deadline, or hidden consequence.
2. Context: the minimum facts needed to understand the issue.
3. Translation: what it means for a seller, operator, or founder.
4. Action: a checklist, decision tree, or four-step process.
5. Comment prompt: a question the viewer can answer from their own experience.

For `30-60s` videos:

- Put the main claim in the first `2-4s`.
- Use one scene per argument.
- Keep each card to one sentence.
- Use at most four list items.
- Save the strongest practical takeaway for the final third.

## Retention

- Open with the consequence, not the background.
- Make each transition answer the question created by the previous scene.
- Repeat a phrase only when it changes emphasis.
- Do not introduce a second unrelated topic.
- Put save value in the middle and the comment prompt at the end.

## Rewriting

Preserve the factual core, names, dates, numbers, and causal claims. Improve:

- the first sentence
- the order of explanation
- the distinction between facts and advice
- the clarity of the final action
- the natural spoken rhythm

Do not fabricate a statistic, quote, product result, or policy change.
