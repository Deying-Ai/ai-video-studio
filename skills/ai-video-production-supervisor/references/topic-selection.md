# Topic Selection

Use this reference when the user wants new topics, a content calendar, or a topic decision before production.

## Required context

Infer when safe, otherwise state the assumption:

- Creator positioning
- Audience and knowledge level
- Audience problem or desired transformation
- Available evidence and real experiences
- Platform and target duration
- Primary goal: reach, saves, comments, follows, or conversion

Do not invent trends. Label an idea as trend-based only when current trend data was inspected.

## Candidate generation

Generate at least 10 distinct candidates across these angles:

1. A frequent, urgent problem.
2. A common misunderstanding.
3. A concrete before-and-after workflow.
4. A tool comparison with a clear decision rule.
5. A mistake and its consequence.
6. A reusable checklist or framework.
7. A real case or project teardown.
8. A counterintuitive but defensible observation.
9. A beginner path with ordered steps.
10. A timely question with evergreen value.

Avoid producing ten versions of the same title.

## Scoring

Score each candidate from 1 to 5:

| Criterion | Weight | What earns a high score |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Audience fit | 25 | Solves a recognized problem for the target audience |
| Hook tension | 20 | Has a clear contradiction, consequence, or unanswered question |
| Save value | 20 | Gives reusable steps, criteria, or a complete method |
| Comment potential | 10 | Invites a meaningful choice or experience, not bait |
| Evidence and credibility | 15 | The creator can support it with experience or sources |
| Production efficiency | 10 | Can be produced clearly with available assets |

Total: 100.

## Output

Write `topic-shortlist.md` with:

- Ten candidates
- One-line thesis for each
- Score breakdown
- Top three recommendations
- Risks or missing evidence
- Recommended primary topic

For each recommendation include:

```text
Topic:
Audience problem:
Promise:
Hook direction:
Proof or experience:
Save value:
Comment prompt:
Production risk:
```

Prefer a strong, provable topic over a dramatic but unsupported one.
